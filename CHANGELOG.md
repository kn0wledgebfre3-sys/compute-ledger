# Changelog

What changed in the data between releases. Figures are identified by `measurement_id`, a hash of their content, so a corrected figure gets a new id; every retired id and the id that replaced it are in `data/climate_trace_seed/measurement_id_aliases.csv`. Every check run on the ledger, and what it found, is in `data/climate_trace_seed/checks.csv` and `findings.csv`.

## Unreleased

- **The ledger as a graph.** Every organisation, country and document now has a page of its own (`docs/org/`, `docs/country/`, `docs/doc/`), and every page of a kind has the same sections in the same order, so what is known and what is not yet found can be compared across companies and countries. An organisation's page gathers the figures about it, its sites, the companies a documented link says belong to its group, its part of the chain, the documents it published, the entries that name it, and the topics with nothing found yet. Each page draws its neighbourhood, and a search box on every page finds any site, organisation, country or document. Built by `pipeline/build_graph.py` and `site/build_graph_pages.py` from the ledger's tables alone; `data/graph/` holds the nodes and links as CSV and as JSON-LD (schema.org, PROV-O, QUDT, SKOS), with the vocabulary at `docs/vocab.html`. The graph sheets are in the workbook, and Explore takes `?org=` and `?doc=`.
- **The chain, drawn.** `chain.html` draws every documented link from refiner and fab to chipmaker, site, power, fuel and water, in five columns, with records, claims and the legs not yet traced told apart; choosing a party follows the chain up and down from it. Site and organisation pages draw their own part of it.
- **Which names are which organisation.** `sites/organisations/` says by hand which organisation each operator name (405), entry (204 links) and publisher (84) names, under the rules in its README. The register now holds 399 organisations, including UK legal entities.
- **Identifiers.** 78 identifiers for 47 organisations (37 LEIs, 41 SEC CIKs), each read on its register page by two readers who agree (`review/organisation_identity_2026-11-17/`). Wikidata items are not included yet: the second reading could not open wikidata.org.
- **Group links.** 47 documented links saying which group a project company or national subsidiary belongs to (Companies House's register of people with significant control, LEI records, SEC subsidiary lists, annual reports, and for a few, regulators' documents or press reports), each read by two readers who agree. They are rows of `sites/chain/edges.csv` (leg `ownership`).
- **United Kingdom.** Seven entries (`registry/europe.json`): Environment Agency and Natural Resources Wales permits for datacentre standby generators, the West London electricity constraint, planning decisions 2023 to 2026, national demand estimates, the datacentre Climate Change Agreement, water companies' plans, and public money for AI compute. 322 figures and 67 sites (`sites/topics/uk/`), each figure read by two readers who agree (`review/uk_round_2026-11-17/`). The sites are not on the map yet.
- **A check of the graph.** 52 links drawn at random were read against their documents (`review/graph_check_2026-11-17/`): 16 as stated and one in part, among the 20 that could be opened. Two roles were corrected: MSFT MCIO Limited is the applicant for the Newport permit, not its operator; and the two Woodlands Park decisions name the appellant company differently ('Altrad Limited' in 2023, 'Altrad UK Limited' in 2025). The other 32 links are to be read in the next check.
- **Qualifiers.** A limit written "shall not ... more than" is now read as a cap, not a floor. Only figures new in this round were affected.
- **UK sites on the map.** 43 of the 67 UK sites are placed, each from the grid reference or easting and northing its permit, decision or report gives, copied by two readers who agree and converted with `pipeline/osgb.py` (tested on the Ordnance Survey's worked example; good to about 5 m) (`review/uk_locations_2026-11-17/`). The site's notes give the reference and the document. Digital Realty Redhill is not placed: its decision document prints a grid reference that falls about 45 km from the Redhill address in the same sentence. Sites with only an address are not placed, and their address is now in their notes.
- **Where the unplaced sites are.** The 185 unplaced sites outside the benchmarking, Virginia and refiner lists were read twice for coordinates or an address (`review/site_locations_2026-11-17/`). No coordinates were found by both readers: most of these sites' documents name only a town or county. Street addresses both readers give are now in 18 sites' notes (Irish and UK datacentres), and one US address joins the geocoding queue. Leads from one reading (a Redhill grid reference that fits its address, Fermi America's power plant, xAI's Tulane Road, CyrusOne DFW10, Ebon at Louisa) wait for a second reading.
- **Placing sites from a table, and geocoding US addresses.** The build now places a site from `sites/locations/placed.csv` (coordinates read from documents) or an accepted row of `sites/locations/geocodes.csv` when its own list has none, and adds the addresses in `sites/locations/addresses.csv` to site notes. `pipeline/address_queue.py` lists the 275 US sites whose record gives a street address (benchmarking filings, Virginia permits, addresses read twice); `pipeline/geocode_addresses.py` asks the US Census geocoder for each, under the ledger's rule (the same house number, street and ZIP). `pipeline/coords.py` converts latitude and longitude as printed, the Irish Grid, ITM and UTM.
- **Plain wording.** Older notes and review files now describe in plain words how pages were read. Three findings whose quoted text changed keep the ids they were published with. A note on the Cheshunt application that stopped mid-sentence now ends.
- **Google's Waltham Cross site.** Its region is now England, with Hertfordshire kept beside the town, as for the other UK sites.
- **What states give up for datacenters.** Six entries from official evaluations and statutory reports on state tax incentives for datacenters, beside the Virginia and Georgia evaluations already held: Minnesota (an evaluation for the legislature: forgone state revenue of $68.7 million in 2024, about $0.98 in value added per $1 forgone, a share of activity attributable to the exemption that fell from 50% to 24%), Washington (two JLARC reviews and Department of Revenue figures; the urban exemption built no new datacenters and JLARC recommends letting it expire), Illinois (the Department of Commerce's annual reports, whose 'value of tax benefits' is a formula, not tax forgone), Ohio, Texas, and eight states whose documents give little or no annual cost (Arizona, Indiana, Oklahoma, North Carolina, South Carolina, Nebraska, Michigan, Nevada). 60 figures, each read by two readers who agree (`review/us_state_incentives_check_2026-11-17/`).
- **EPA inventory facilities.** A second search identified 21 of the 23 facilities outside Virginia not yet identified; two remain (`review/us_nei_identity_round2_2026-11-17/`).

## 1.0.0 (2026-11-16)

First citable release. 203 entries in 44 countries and territories plus EU-wide and global entries; 3,899 figures (663 of them claims); 917 sites, 367 of them placed from a document; 207 documented links in the chain; 727 distinct linked documents; 23 checks and 1,560 findings.

Changes since the version shared on 26 October 2026:

- **Ireland, 2023.** The ten 2023 Annual Environmental Reports of the licensed datacentres (eight Amazon licences, Microsoft DUB14/15, Equinix DB5) are read and added: 39 figures. They cover part of the year; the period each report states is recorded in a new `period_start` column of `sites/metered/ireland_aer_transcribed.csv` (with the wording in `period_basis`) and used for average load. For the eight Amazon licences, average load was 370.6 MW from the month of licence grant (June or July 2023) to December, 377.6 MW in 2024 and 412.0 MW in 2025 (398.5 MW for 2023 if counted from the date of grant; `metered_summary.json`). P1181-01's 2024 average load is withdrawn: its report says it covers 8 August to 31 December "unless stated otherwise", but its Table 3 says "the energy used in 2024". Read from the rendered pages against the weekly pull's text extraction, then checked against the PDFs by a second reader; one figure the extraction missed (P1170-01's HVO) was added.
- **NOx units.** EPA's emissions inventory reports US short tons; the text had written them as `t` (tonnes), about 10% high. The text now says short tons (393 short tons of NOx at 27 Virginia facilities in 2023 is 357 tonnes). The figures' converted values were already right. Twelve figures' wording changed, so their ids changed; see the aliases file.
- **EPA's emissions inventory beyond Virginia.** The first weekly pull's 71 facilities in 13 more states that a state classed under NAICS 518210 (computing infrastructure, data processing, web hosting), 2020 and 2023: 663 figures (NOx, CO, VOC, SO2, PM10, PM2.5), first readings by script, in `sites/metered/us_nei_transcribed.csv`, and a new entry, `us-nei-datacenter-facilities-2020-2023`. What and where each facility is was checked by three readers from other documents (`review/us_nei_identity_check_2026-11-02/`): 47 identified, 24 not yet; six inventory addresses are an owner's mailing address in another state. 62 new sites are placed by the inventory's coordinates; nine facilities were placed on sites already listed, only where a document says they are the same. For 12 Virginia facilities, the other criteria pollutants were added beside their NOx (51 figures).
- **Benchmarking, earlier years.** 193 building-years from the weekly pull, back to 2011, for San Francisco, Seattle, Cambridge, Chicago, Washington DC and New York (151 figures; nine rows left out, with reasons, in `sites/metered/benchmarking_notes.md`).
- **Irish licences.** A reader checked the 15 licence numbers the pull found outside the ledger's list (`review/ireland_licences_check_2026-11-02/`). EPA documents, then EPA's own licence register (as returned to the pull of 16 November), show four more licences in force since 2023 (Microsoft P1190-01 and P1191-01, Google P1189-01, Runways Information Services P1192-01) and three granted since (K2 P1143-01, Echelon P1113-02, EdgeConneX P1204-01), so the statement that the ledger's 11 were all of Ireland's licensed datacentres was withdrawn. Their annual reports are not read yet; all seven are now on the pull's watch list. The register also gives Amazon's Leixlip application P1222-01 the status 'Compliance Failure/Rejected' and lists a new one, P1238-01. The pull now records each unlisted licence's profile.
- **Explore, map and workbook.** `explore.html` searches and filters every figure, downloads the rows chosen as CSV (the columns of `measurements.csv`), and gives each figure a permanent link and a citation; a retired id leads to the corrected figure. `map.html` shows the 305 sites placed from a document, with the full list of 853 below it. `data/compute-ledger.xlsx` holds every table on its own sheet with a sheet describing the columns. All three are built by `site/build_explore.py` and checked in CI. Corrections and new records go through two GitHub issue forms, linked from each figure.
- **Data package.** `data/datapackage.json` describes every table and its columns; `data/DICTIONARY.md` is its readable version; every build checks every table against it. The entries are now also tables: `entries.csv` and `entry_sources.csv`.
- **Weekly pulls.** The first live weekly pull (2026-10-26) is kept in `data/pulls/2026-10-26/` with a note on how to read it; it changes no table until each item is read against its document.
- **Documentation.** The README starts with where the data is and how to cite it; the contribution steps now pass the checks as written; adding a country or topic is documented in `JURISDICTION_TEMPLATE.md`; `ci_local.sh` now fails where CI would; stale files moved to `history/`.
- **Licence.** 30 seed-list rows rest on a Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA 4.0) for the site's identity or location; they are now flagged in the README and their licence value is listed in the asset file's definitions.
