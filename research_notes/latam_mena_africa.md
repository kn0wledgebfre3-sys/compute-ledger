## Key Question 1: Chile, Cerrillos (Google): what the court said, what the EIA stated about water, what the revised design is

### Takeaway
On 26 Feb 2024 the Segundo Tribunal Ambiental de Santiago partially annulled the 2020 environmental approval (RCA Res. Ex. N° 127, 24 Feb 2020) of Google subsidiary Dataluna's "Cerrillos Data Center" because the SEA had not justified its finding of no significant impact on the Acuífero Santiago Central and had not incorporated climate-change effects; the original DIA described groundwater extraction of up to 169 L/s from purchased rights in an aquifer under prohibition. In Sept 2024 Google told the SEA it would abandon the approved project and start a new air-cooled design "from scratch"; as of the sources found, no new submission is documented.

### Cited Findings
- Ruling dated 26 February 2024, Santiago; case R-271-2020 with R-270-2020 joined (acumulada); RCA is Resolución Exenta N° 127 of 24 Feb 2020 issued by COEVA Región Metropolitana; project owner "Inversiones y Servicios Dataluna Ltda."; invalidation request rejected by Resolución Exenta N° 524 of 28 Oct 2020. The 2022 consultation (Res. Ex. N° 202213101388, 7 June 2022) changed cooling from water towers to air condensers, eliminating groundwater use from three wells with water rights — [Sentencia R-271-2020 acum. R-270-2020, Segundo Tribunal Ambiental (PDF mirror at El Mostrador)](https://media-front.elmostrador.cl/2024/02/2024.02.26_Sentencia_R-271-2020_Acum._R_N°_270-2020.pdf)
- Court orders: SEA must roll back (retrotraer) the evaluation to incorporate climate-change effects into the water component, issue a new ICSARA, obtain agency opinions, prepare a new ICE and issue a complementary RCA; the un-annulled part is suspended until the complementary RCA is issued. Panel: Cristián Delpiano Lira (redactor), Cristián López Montecinos, María Merino Verdugo. Claim originally filed by 14 neighbours and the Municipality of Cerrillos; 13 neighbours and the municipality later withdrew, leaving one resident — [BioBioChile, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/nacional/region-metropolitana/2024/02/27/anulan-parcialmente-permiso-de-data-center-de-google-piden-que-incluya-efectos-de-cambio-climatico.shtml)
- The tribunal found the RCA and Res. Ex. 524/2020 "incurren en ilegalidad" over lack of evidence of no significant impact on the Acuífero Santiago Central; the project conflicts with the precautionary principle; DGA measures do not replace a proper assessment — [El Mostrador, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.elmostrador.cl/noticias/pais/2024/02/27/tribunal-pone-freno-a-google-data-center-debe-incorporar-cambio-climatico-en-evaluacion-ambiental/)
- [older, 2020] Original design: water consumption of 169 litres per second at maximum capacity, continuous; water rights purchased from Huturi (51 and 52 L/s) and Lo Prado S.A./SEPRA (125 L/s) in the Santiago Central aquifer sector; the sector has been under a restriction decree since 2005 and a "zona de prohibición" for new groundwater extraction since 1 Feb 2020; RCA approved 25 Feb 2020; ~US$200 million; 79,216 m² building on ~29 ha next to Plaza Oeste mall — [CIPER Chile, 25 May 2020](https://www.ciperchile.cl/?p=138241)
- Google statement after the ruling (Feb 2024): it had already submitted a change to its original design to use air cooling and would cooperate with authorities; article is dated 29 Feb 2024 — [DCD (es), 29 Feb 2024](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/es/noticias/chile-revoca-parcialmente-los-permisos-al-nuevo-centro-de-datos-de-google-por-preocupaciones-sobre-uso-de-agua)
- Sept 2024: Google told the SEA it "no seguirá adelante con el proceso de solicitud de los permisos"; it will draw up a new environmental plan from scratch; 2020 commitment to air-condensation cooling noted; project ~US$200 million — [Bloomberg Línea, 17 Sep 2024](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/chile/google-anuncia-que-reformulara-plan-para-data-center-en-chile-por-impacto-ambiental/)
- Google (via El Mercurio): will not proceed "tal como fue presentado originalmente y aprobado en 2020"; a new process "desde cero" for an air-cooled project at the same site, timing to be set later; Dataluna informed the SEA Regional Directorate RM; Google cites its goal of 24/7 carbon-free energy — [CNN Chile, Sep 2024](https://www.cnnchile.com/negocios/google-se-rinde-con-proyecto-original-de-data-center-en-cerrillos-y-anuncia-futuro-rediseno-de-la-iniciativa_20240917/)
- Bloomberg Línea (2025) attributes a "7.6 million litres of groundwater per day" figure to the Chile project citing the Segundo Tribunal Ambiental; this conflicts with other sources that attribute 7.6 million L/day to the Uruguay project (see KQ2). Treat the Chile attribution as unverified — [Bloomberg Línea, 2025](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/tecnologia/google-adapta-centros-de-datos-en-latam-ante-retos-hidricos-este-fue-el-caso-de-uruguay/)

### Inferences
- 169 L/s continuous equals roughly 14,600 m³/day (arithmetic on the CIPER figure; the court record fetched does not restate a daily figure).
- The ruling is a documented case of a court forcing climate-change consideration into a data-center water assessment; the operator's subsequent withdrawal makes it also a "redesign after objection" case, though the redesign is not yet on the public record.

### Gaps
- The full operative section ("resuelve") and water chapter of the ruling could not be fetched (PDF excerpt truncated); the Tribunal's own site (tribunalambiental.cl) was not reached. The case number and dates are from the PDF header.
- No SEA record of any new Google/Dataluna submission for Cerrillos found as of Oct 2026.

## Key Question 1b: Chile national data center plan

### Takeaway
The Ministry of Science's Plan Nacional de Data Centers (public consultation 29 Oct–20 Nov 2024; presented 5 Dec 2024) counts 22 medium/large data centers in operation, proposes Clean Production Agreements (APL) on water/energy efficiency, and AI campuses in regions with high renewable availability, but contains no MW totals, no water-use limits and no cooling requirements.

### Cited Findings
- "Chile cuenta con 22 Data Centers de mediana y gran escala en operación"; Measure 5: Acuerdos de Producción Limpia to improve water and energy efficiency and reduce carbon footprint; proposes AI technology campuses "en regiones con alta disponibilidad de energías renovables"; public consultation 29 Oct–20 Nov 2024; no MW, no investment totals, no cooling guidance — [Plan Nacional de Data Centers, MinCiencia (PDF mirror)](https://guerrero.cl/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/DOC_Plan-Nacional-Data-Centers.pdf)
- Presented 5 Dec 2024; targets include at least one AI campus built in defined regions (2028–2030); APL implemented 2025–2027 with a sustainability standard to follow 2028–2030; IDE Chile geospatial tool weighs energy availability and socio-environmental variables — [Carey (law firm summary), 26 Dec 2024](https://www.carey.cl/en/launch-of-the-national-data-centers-plan-2024-2030)

### Inferences
- The plan is a policy framework, not a permitting regime; water and energy sustainability is voluntary (APL) in the 2025–2027 window.

### Gaps
- Official minciencia.gob.cl hosting of the plan not fetched directly (link found but not opened); a mirror was used.

## Key Question 2: Uruguay, Canelones (Google): released documents, drought context, revised cooling

### Takeaway
A public-information request by researcher Daniel Pena (Cndav) led the Juzgado Letrado en lo Contencioso Administrativo and then the Tribunal de Apelaciones en lo Civil de 7° Turno (sentence 23 Feb 2023) to order the Ministry of Environment to release the project's potable-water need, which the ministry had withheld as industrial secrecy; released figures were 7,600 m³/day (7.6 million L/day) in the original design and a Google-stated first-stage peak of 3,800 m³/day, during Uruguay's 2023 drinking-water crisis. Google then redesigned the project with 32 air-cooled chillers in closed loop and a stated 48 m³/day water use; the AAP documentation was filed 26 Oct 2023.

### Cited Findings
- Feb 2023: Daniel Pena (adjunct researcher, Dept. of Sociology, Facultad de Ciencias Sociales) and Cndav members sought the water volume of project "Teros"; the Ministerio de Ambiente refused citing "reserva industrial"; the Tribunal en lo Contencioso Administrativo ruled for the plaintiffs; ministry appealed — [El Observador, 17 Feb 2023](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/nota/el-agua-que-google-necesitara-en-uruguay-para-su-data-center-desata-una-pelea-judicial-2023217143847)
- Tribunal de Apelaciones en lo Civil de 7° Turno confirmed the first-instance order (sentence of 23 February); ministry must deliver the volume of potable water needed by the data center, operating as Eleanor Applications SRL; ministry had cited "secreto industrial o comercial" — [El Observador, Mar 2023](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/nota/uso-de-agua-en-data-center-de-google-tribunal-ordena-a-ambiente-entregar-datos-202331203016/amp)
- Google's March 2023 response to the court order gave a first-stage peak of 3,800 m³/day (3.8 million L); 18 May 2023: Environment Minister Robert Bouvier said the original project "se retiró" and "hoy por hoy no hay proyecto"; Ministry of Industry said the project "sigue en pie" and is being "reformulada" with "un menor uso de agua" — [El Observador, 18 May 2023](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/nota/ministro-de-ambiente-dijo-que-plan-de-data-center-de-google-se-retiro-hoy-por-hoy-no-tenemos-proyecto--202351893243)
- Ministry estimate obtained via court action: 7.6 million L/day from the public network, "enough for 55,000 people"; drought context: Paso Severino reservoir supplies ~2 million people; government mixed in brackish Río de la Plata water; tap water had about twice normal sodium and three times normal chloride; 29 ha site — [Agência Pública, Aug 2023](https://apublica.org/2023/08/a-briga-dos-uruguaios-contra-o-google/)
- Revised design (Nov 2023 EIA documentation): original plan 7,600 m³/day potable water (≈55,000 people); revised plan 48 m³/day for kitchens and bathrooms; cooling via 32 air-cooled chillers in closed water circuits (7+1 per module), with the largest water draw at initial fill; one building instead of two, reducing capacity to one third of original; 44-month build in four stages; power from UTE via on-site substation plus backup generators; ministry asked for maximum energy consumption and confirmation that demand will not affect UTE supply quality to third parties, and for backup-engine emission data sheets — [El Observador, 22 Nov 2023](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/nota/sin-agua-con-enfriamiento-por-aire-y-un-edificio-en-lugar-de-dos-los-detalles-del-proyecto-de-google-en-uruguay-2023112116100)
- Google (Alberto Oppenheimer, Google Cloud LatAm), 7 Nov 2023: servers will be air-cooled; water use comparable to ordinary commercial/domestic and fire-protection needs; AAP (Autorización Ambiental Previa) documentation submitted 26 Oct 2023 — [El Observador, 7 Nov 2023](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/nota/data-center-de-google-en-uruguay-enfriara-servidores-con-aire-sin-uso-intensivo-de-agua-202311619530)
- Google (Eduardo López, 2025): Uruguay "se analizó como data center alternativo" when Chile's project was in question; architecture reformulated to cool with air; investment >US$850 million; Uruguay's energy matrix >90% renewable — [Bloomberg Línea, 2025](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/tecnologia/google-adapta-centros-de-datos-en-latam-ante-retos-hidricos-este-fue-el-caso-de-uruguay/)

### Inferences
- The reduction from 7,600 m³/day to 48 m³/day is roughly a 99% cut in stated operational water use, achieved by switching to closed-loop air-cooled chillers; this is a documented redesign-after-disclosure case.
- Discrepancy: 7,600 m³/day (ministry estimate, full original design) vs 3,800 m³/day (Google's "first stage peak") are not contradictory if the latter is phase 1 of the former, but sources do not reconcile them.

### Gaps
- The ministry's released document itself and the court sentence text were not fetched; figures are via established Uruguayan press and Agência Pública.
- No MW figure for the Canelones site found in any source; the "200,000 households" energy claim circulating in press was not traced to a primary document.
- Date of the AAP grant not found.

## Key Question 3: Gulf — Abu Dhabi 5 GW campus and Saudi projects: power sourcing and water

### Takeaway
The only official statement found on power sourcing for the Abu Dhabi UAE–US AI Campus (5 GW, unveiled 15 May 2025) is the UAE government release that it "will use nuclear, solar, and gas power"; no DEWA/EWEC/Masdar document and no water/desalination disclosure was found. For Saudi Arabia, Humain (PIF subsidiary, launched May 2025) states capacity targets (1.9 GW by 2030, ~6 GW over a decade, 100 MW each in Riyadh and Dammam) and holds a Sept 2026 cooperation agreement with National Grid SA on grid supply to a Riyadh AI data center, but no public document specifies generation mix or water.

### Cited Findings
- UAE–US AI Campus, Abu Dhabi, unveiled 15 May 2025: 5 GW of AI data-center capacity; built by G42; access jointly regulated by UAE and US, reserved for US hyperscalers and approved cloud providers; "will use nuclear, solar, and gas power to minimise carbon emissions once completed"; no water or cooling statement — [UAE Embassy in Washington (reproducing WAM), 15 May 2025](https://www.uae-embassy.org/news/uae-us-presidents-attend-unveiling-phase-1-new-5gw-ai-campus-abu-dhabi)
- Stargate UAE: 1 GW cluster within the 5 GW campus; builder Khazna Data Centers (G42 subsidiary); first 200 MW on accelerated timeline, "planned 2026 delivery"; all long-lead equipment procured as of Oct 2025; no power-supply or cooling details — [Gulf Business, 19 Oct 2025](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2025/tech/g42-announces-major-progress-on-stargate-uae/)
- Humain (PIF subsidiary launched May 2025): CEO Tareq Amin targets 1.9 GW by 2030 rising to 6.6 GW ~2034; initial 50 MW facility with 18,000 Nvidia GPUs scaling to 500 MW; 2.3 sq mi Eastern Province land for ten 200 MW data centers; Riyadh "will subsidize electricity prices" (no figure); no power source named — [DCD, 28 May 2025, citing FT interview](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/saudi-arabias-ai-co-humain-looking-for-us-data-center-equity-partner-targets-66gw-by-2034-with-subsidized-electricity)
- Humain confirmed 211 land plots across Saudi Arabia; ~6 GW planned over the next decade; first data centers in Riyadh and Dammam at 100 MW each expected live Q2 2026; no power source, water or desalination details — [DCD, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/humain-secures-211-plots-of-land-in-saudi-arabia-for-data-centers/)
- 2 Sep 2026 (LEAP 2026): National Grid SA and HUMAIN signed a cooperation agreement on supplying electricity to an AI data center project in Riyadh covering electrical infrastructure and future power needs; Saudi Electricity Project Development Company (PDC) signed a framework with center3 and an MoU with Huawei; no MW, pricing or generation source stated — [TradeArabia/OGN, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.tradearabia.com/News/467418/Saudi-Energy-signs-three-agreements-to-support-AI-data-centre-growth/OGN)
- Egypt–UAE context: Khazna Maadi facility (US$250 m) is part of an Egypt–UAE agreement for up to 1 GW in phases (see KQ7) — [Enterprise (Egypt), 9 Sep 2026](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/hardhats/our-data-center-boom-is-waiting-on-a-switch/)

### Inferences
- The official UAE statement lists gas alongside nuclear and solar; it does not give shares, so the campus's carbon profile cannot be computed from the public record.
- For Saudi Arabia, the public record consists of capacity targets and grid-cooperation agreements; water is entirely absent from the documents found.

### Gaps
- No EWEC, DEWA, ADWEA, Masdar, ENEC/Barakah, or Abu Dhabi DoE document tying specific generation or desalinated water to the campus was found.
- No SEC/ECRA (Saudi Electricity Regulatory Authority) filing or tariff document for Humain found; "subsidized electricity" is a press-reported CEO claim.
- Qatar: no primary record found within budget.

## Key Question 3b: Israel grid constraints

### Takeaway
On 20 July 2026 Israel's Electricity Authority froze processing of new grid-connection applications for data centers of 8 MW or more for 140 days (to early December 2026), after transmission operator Noga received ~19 GW of new requests in two months on top of an ~8 GW backlog (≈27 GW total, about three times average national consumption of ~9 GW).

### Cited Findings
- Announced Monday 20 July 2026; 140-day freeze until early December; applies to ≥8 MW; public consultation open until 19 Aug; ~19 GW new requests in two months; ~8 GW existing backlog; ~27 GW pending; ~1.5 GW existing commitments already consume planned capacity through 2035; Israel average consumption ~9 GW, peak ~17 GW (Aug 2025); Noga had approved ~2 GW of connections; Electricity Authority head Amir Shavit; Energy Ministry DG Yossi Dayan — [Calcalist/CTech, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/tvp6729tr)
- Authority called it an "emergency brake" to avoid commitments it "may be unable to fulfill"; surge followed the National Cyber Defense Bill (passed by Knesset in February 2026) classifying data centers as national infrastructure; joint review by Electricity Authority, Ministry of Energy and Noga to set allocation policy; VisionWave dropped hyperscale plans citing the pause — [DCD, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/israeli-electrical-authority-freezes-grid-processing-of-new-data-centers-for-140-days/)

### Inferences
- The freeze is a regulator-imposed moratorium on new connection processing, not on construction of already-approved sites.

### Gaps
- The Electricity Authority's own consultation document was not fetched; figures are via Calcalist and DCD.

## Key Question 4: South Africa — Eskom constraints, on-site diesel/gas, water permits

### Takeaway
Teraco (186 MW critical load across Isando, Bredell, Cape Town, Durban) reported grid availability "around 15%" at some sites during 2022–2023 load-shedding and "millions of rands a month" in diesel spend, and in Feb 2024 announced a 120 MW Free State solar plant to be wheeled across Eskom and municipal networks after its first Eskom grid-capacity allocation. In Cape Town, four new projects (Cavaleros 360 MW, Teraco +60 MW, Equinix 160 MW peak) total ~580 MW, about 34% of the city's 1,676 MW maximum demand; objectors filed 13 Apr 2026 noting no water or diesel-storage disclosure, while Equinix says its new halls use dry air cooling with "no water for data hall cooling."

### Cited Findings
- Teraco: 120 MW solar PV in Free State, ~R2 billion, >338,000 MWh/yr, wheeled across Eskom and municipal networks to Teraco sites nationally; first Eskom grid-capacity allocation secured; critical power load 186 MW (Isando 70, Bredell 64, Cape Town 50, Durban 2); CEO Jan Hnizdo: grid availability "around 15%" at some sites, diesel spend "millions of rands in a month" — [Techpoint Africa, 21 Feb 2024](https://techpoint.africa/2024/02/21/teraco-build-solar-facility-south-africas)
- Cape Town: Cavaleros 360 MW (announced 2025), Teraco CT1 Rondebosch +60 MW, Equinix two sites 160 MW combined peak (Equinix: "maximum-case scenario"); total 580 MW ≈ 34% of City maximum demand 1,676 MW (Daily Maverick calculation; City did not comment); City declined demand projections and backup-generator data citing POPIA; City "promotes treated effluent for high-consumption users like data centres"; Equinix: dry air-based cooling, "no water for data hall cooling"; objection by Foxglove and Housing Assembly (Legal Resources Centre) filed 13 Apr 2026 citing undisclosed water volumes, generator numbers, diesel storage and Major Hazard Installation risk — [Daily Maverick, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-28-new-data-centres-set-to-swallow-34-of-cape-towns-current-electricity-supply/)
- [older, context] Western Cape Government briefing to NCOP, 14 Feb 2023: "over 3000 hours of any stage of load shedding during 2022" (>200 days); Stage 6 reached in 2023; City of Cape Town can reduce stages 05:00–22:00 via Steenbras pumped storage; diesel supply becomes constrained at higher stages — [Western Cape Government / Parliament of RSA, 14 Feb 2023](https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Pages/2023/14-02-2023_NCOP_Ministerial_Briefing_Session_on_the_Impact_of_Loadshedding/session2/Western_Cape.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Teraco solar/wheeling case is a documented positive example (renewable procurement via grid wheeling) that arose directly from load-shedding-driven diesel dependence.
- The Cape Town case shows municipal process (rezoning under the planning by-law) rather than a water permit record; water volumes for all four projects are not on the public record.

### Gaps
- No NERSA licence, Eskom wheeling agreement, or Department of Water and Sanitation water-use licence for any SA data center was located.
- Amazon and Microsoft Cape Town/Gauteng site-level power or water figures not found in official records.

## Key Question 5: Kenya — geothermal data center project status and documented figures

### Takeaway
Two geothermal-fed projects exist at KenGen's Olkaria Green Energy Park: EcoCloud "Project Eagle" (ground broken 1 Sep 2023; phase 1 24 MW IT across eight 3 MW halls, 70 MW total draw, 7 US cents/kWh from KenGen, air-cooled chilled water, design PUE ≤1.25; MoU with G42 in Mar 2024 to scale 100 MW→1 GW) and the Microsoft–G42 US$1 billion campus (KenGen release 23 May 2024; 100 MW initial, East Africa cloud region "within 24 months"). By May 2026 President Ruto said the Microsoft–G42 project would need ~1 GW against Kenya's ~3 GW installed capacity ("we would need to shut off power for half the country"), the May 2026 launch date passed, and the project is reported stalled.

### Cited Findings
- [older, 2023] EcoCloud Project Eagle groundbreaking, 1 Sep 2023, KenGen Green Energy Park, Olkaria: phase 1 24 MW IT across eight 3 MW halls (300–500 racks each); total expected consumption 70 MW; power at 7 US cents/kWh from KenGen; air-cooled chilled-water network, PUE ≤1.25; consultants Sudlows and X2X; attended by Prime CS Musalia Mudavadi and CSs Miano, Owalo, Chirchir — [DCD, 1 Sep 2023](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ground-broken-on-geothermal-powered-data-center-in-kenya)
- EcoCloud–G42 MoU, 8 Mar 2024: campus to start at 100 MW with potential to 1 GW; phase 1 24 MW IT; signed by Amos Siwoi and Peng Xiao before President Ruto — [DCD, 8 Mar 2024](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/uaes-g42-partners-with-kenyas-ecocloud-for-geothermal-data-center)
- KenGen release, 23 May 2024: US$1 billion Microsoft–G42 data center campus at the 342 ha Green Energy Park; "100% renewable geothermal"; Olkaria fields ~800 MW; groundbreaking planned Q3 2024; East Africa Cloud Region live within 24 months; KenGen geothermal 754 MW, total 1,786 MW — [KenGen, 23 May 2024](https://www.kengen.co.ke/kengen-teams-up-with-microsoft-and-g42-for-1b-cutting-edge-green-data-center-in-olkaria/)
- May 2026: Ruto: "To switch on that one data center, we would need to shut off power for half the country"; proposed 1 GW vs Kenya supply 3 GW; concept note to National Treasury not cleared; Aug 2025 officials acknowledged May 2026 date would be missed; Bloomberg (10 May 2026): breakdown stemmed from Microsoft/G42 request that government commit to lease set annual capacity; original figures: May 2024 announcement, US$1bn, 100 MW initial, Microsoft promised "state-of-the-art water conservation technology"; Airtel Nxtra 44 MW (Tatu City) and EcoCloud Project Eagle continue — [DCD, 7 May 2026](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-and-g42-data-center-in-kenya-stalled-due-to-lack-of-power-capacity/)
- ThinkGeoEnergy, 8 May 2026: Ruto announced suspension; Treasury declined to fund concept note; Ruto goal 10,000 MW — [ThinkGeoEnergy, 8 May 2026](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/kenya-suspends-plans-for-microsofts-geothermal-powered-data-centre/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- Discrepancy: KenGen's 2024 release and DCD describe a 100 MW initial campus; Ruto's 2026 statement frames the ask as ~1 GW. The 1 GW figure matches the EcoCloud–G42 scale-up MoU ceiling; sources do not reconcile which project the 1 GW refers to.
- EcoCloud Project Eagle is a documented geothermal-fed, air-cooled site with a disclosed tariff and PUE target; status beyond groundbreaking is not documented in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) or KenGen PPA document for either project located.
- Current operating status of EcoCloud Project Eagle (MW energised) not found.

## Key Question 6: Brazil — grid share, site approvals, water records

### Takeaway
ONS said on 4 Sep 2025 it had issued access opinions for 30 data centers totalling ~2.5 GW (12 on the basic network, 7 on other transmission, 11 at generation plants), mostly in São Paulo; EPE counts 26.2 GW of data-center projects in the connection process at end-2025 and ~8.3 GW across 30 Northeast projects to 2038. Site cases: Scala AI City, Eldorado do Sul (MME-authorised 5 GW ramp, 4.75 GW at full build, phase 1 54 MW, licensed under a municipal "simplified and self-declaratory" regime with no state data-center licensing category); Casa dos Ventos/ByteDance "Data Center Pecém" (ONS connection approved May 2025 after an earlier rejection; 300 MW phase 1; RAS-based state licence with 18–30 m³/day stated water use, two SRH outorgas and an Aug 2025 reuse-water agreement; MPF complaint filed Oct 2025). The Redata tax regime (MP 1.318/2025) lapsed on 25 Feb 2026 without a Senate vote.

### Cited Findings
- ONS DG Marcio Rea, ANEEL workshop, 4 Sep 2025: 30 data centers with accepted access opinions ≈2.5 GW on the SIN (12 rede básica, 7 other transmission, 11 at generation plants); 4 more under analysis in H2 2025; concentration in São Paulo; 2025 transmission auction lot for Barueri/Santana do Parnaíba/São Paulo; EPE projects ~50 projects ≈14 GW — [Agência iNFRA, 4 Sep 2025](https://agenciainfra.com/blog/ons-ja-autorizou-conexao-de-30-data-centers-diz-diretor-geral/)
