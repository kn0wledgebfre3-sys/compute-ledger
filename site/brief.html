A brief for the Climate TRACE coalition · 23 October 2026

Datacenters as a facility type: the paper layer imagery can't see

You estimate what power plants emit. A datacenter has no chimney; its footprint is the plant that serves it, and which plant that is sits in utility dockets, air permits and licence applications. The Compute Ledger has been collecting those documents, site by site, in 38 countries and territories. This page is what they give you, with the limits stated.

Ireland: a site list with permit-grade coordinates, and the test any estimate must pass

Ireland is the one country we know of whose statistics office publishes a metered national datacenter electricity figure: 7,663 GWh in 2025, 23% of metered consumption (CSO). Nobody keeps a site register, so we built one from EPA industrial-emissions licences, EU ETS permits and planning appeals: 40 sites, 22 located, most of them from grid references inside the EPA documents themselves (per-stack coordinates in licence attachments, site-centre points on application forms, main-entrance references on older permits). Sixteen operating sites carry permitted standby generation: 3,830 MW thermal by licence cap, 4,279 by sum of units.

Generators are sized to carry a site's full design load, so permitted standby bounds load from above once the redundancy design is known. That design is the uncertain factor. Under N+1 the metered total bounds utilisation of permitted capacity at roughly 0.54–0.71 and plausibly puts it at 0.3–0.5; under 2N it imposes no bound.

The regulator's own meters have now been read. Every EPA-licensed installation files an Annual Environmental Report with metered electricity, water and generator fuel. The eleven licensed installations (nine Amazon, Microsoft DUB14/15, Equinix DB5) report 4,109 GWh for 2025, 53.6% of the CSO national total, Amazon's nine alone 48.5%; 3,585 GWh (51.4%) for 2024. The largest, Amazon Clonshaugh, drew 1,020 GWh, a 116 MW average. Generator fuel at the Amazon sites equals 0.6 to 6.4 hours a year of the whole permitted fleet at full rating. At Equinix DB5 the 2024 report records ten months of running the generators as prime power on gas while the grid connection was late: 510,550 GJ of gas against 303,804 GJ of electricity.

What this gives a datacenter subsector: a located site list in a metered country, a permit-derived capacity ceiling per site, metered annual load from the regulator for the eleven sites that carry just over half of the national total, and a national total the other 29 must sum to. What it does not give: the per-site split of that other half, and generator run hours, which the reports do not state.

Virginia: the permits, extracted

Virginia DEQ publishes a list of every issued datacenter air permit. On 21 October we downloaded the permits on the list and extracted the standby generator inventory from each of the 182 permit documents, then read every permit a second time with an independent method and resolved the disagreements by hand: 182 permits, 13,973 permitted standby generator units, 33,693 MW, 177 at high confidence, median permit 129 MW, largest 1,331 MW; 180 with a site address in the resolved file, 102 geocoded to that address and placed in the site file. Site-average unit size runs 0.4 to 3.7 MW, median 2.5, with most units at 2 to 3 MW. The first automated reading had given 19,186 MW; the second reading found it low in 98 of 107 disagreements, traced most of the gap to one parser bug (the first row after each table header dropped) and the rest to seven smaller patterns, and resolved every case against the permit text. The comparison, the resolutions and the error patterns are in the package.

What the utilities file is the other half of the check. Dominion's 20-year datacenter forecast to PJM (29 September 2026) gives datacenter billing demand of 4,237 MW in 2025 (2,767 in 2022), a 2025 coincident peak of 4 GW, 55 companies, about 500 metered accounts, and 'approximately 54GW of capacity under contract'; its IRP update to the SCC filed 47,045 MW under contract at end-July 2025, of which 16,913 MW under firm contracts and 30,132 MW still being studied at the engineering-letter stage. NOVEC's submission adds 1,384 MW of coincident peak and says datacenters are 75% of its energy sales. So about 5.6 GW was drawing power in 2025, against 33.7 GW of permitted standby and 47 to 54 GW under contract: the permits describe what is planned, the filings what is running. Standby is sized to full design load with redundancy, so 33.7 GW permitted implies a design load of roughly 28–33 GW under N+1 and about half that under 2N. No filing gives annual GWh; Dominion's 10-K puts datacenters at 28% of its Virginia sales in 2025, a company figure. That is the Ireland check in the one region where you would pilot: a public ceiling per site from a document, a filed demand total it must respect, and both checked twice.

What the permitted generators actually emit is in EPA's 2023 National Emissions Inventory: 27 Virginia datacenter facilities, 393 tons of NOx, the largest Amazon IAD-132/133 at 77.6 t and Microsoft Boydton at 28.8 t (20.8 t in 2020). The inventory reports emissions, not hours; converting needs each permit's per-unit NOx rate, which the permits state and the extractor will read next.

The two numbers every operator reports

Your estimates are physical. The operators' headline emissions are not: each reports Scope 2 twice, location-based (the grids' average emissions where its datacenters draw power) and market-based (after renewable certificates, power purchase agreements and supplier contracts). The GHG Protocol's 2015 guidance requires both, the market-based number carries the headlines, and the gap between them is what the paper instruments account for. Crypto mining: the same grids, often the same sites

Mining shares datacenters' queues and permits and is now handing its sites to AI tenants, so the ledger carries it as its own leg. The federal count stopped in 2024: EIA's emergency survey of mining electricity was halted by a temporary restraining order and withdrawn, leaving its estimate of 0.6% to 2.3% of US electricity. Texas requires mining sites over 75 MW to register with the PUCT, but the adoption order puts registrations in a tool "that will not be accessible to the public". What is public comes from SEC filings: Riot's statement of operations shows $56.7 million of power curtailment credits for 2025, and mining companies have filed AI leases on their sites, among them about 590 MW at Core Scientific for CoreWeave, 378 MW at TeraWulf's Lake Mariner, 300 MW at Cipher for AWS and 245 MW at Hut 8's River Bend. 45 mining and converted sites are in the site register with their filed MW; none yet has a coordinate from a record.

Every figure in one table, kept current

All 2,416 figures in the ledger, from permits, regulators' reports, utility filings, emissions inventories, benchmarking laws, assured company schedules and the entries' own text, sit in one table with one vocabulary of metrics and units, a canonical unit per dimension, each figure exactly as it is written in the text it was taken from, the source link, and whether a second reading has confirmed it. The table is rebuilt and validated on every change to the repository; a figure taken from an entry must appear in that entry word for word, and its source must be one the entry lists. A weekly job pulls EPA's emissions inventory in 16 states, Ireland's licensing system and eight city benchmarking portals, watches Virginia's permit list, PJM's load-forecast materials and utility pages, and opens anything new for review. Building it found 75 figures whose document was missing from their entry's sources and one percentage paired with the wrong base. A reviewer who had not seen the build then checked 56 figures drawn at random against their documents: 53 were there as stated, and the problems found in the rest and in the rows' logic (fiscal years given as calendar years, three gas plants labelled standby, company figures filed as records) are corrected wherever the pattern occurred. Both are listed in the repository.

Method, in one paragraph

Site emissions = load × [α · EFdedicated + (1 − α) · EFgrid], reported as a range with a confidence rating. That is your 5.11.0 facility Scope 2 pattern with one added field. Load must come from a signal independent of the supply document (permit generator MW, interconnection MW, register IT load, footprint), never from the supply MW itself; dedicated supply counts only from the year it is physically in service; the result is Scope 2, non-additive to your power-sector rows. We drafted five worked rows and withdrew them after review because they broke the first of those rules. The method note and the review are both in the package.
