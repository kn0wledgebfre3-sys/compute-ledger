Datacenters as a facility type: the paper layer imagery can't see

You estimate what power plants emit. A datacenter has no chimney; its footprint is the plant that serves it, and which plant that is sits in utility dockets, air permits and licence applications. The Compute Ledger has been collecting those documents, site by site, in 40 countries. This page is what they give you, with the limits stated.

Ireland: a site list with permit-grade coordinates, and the test any estimate must pass

Ireland is the one country we know of whose statistics office publishes a metered national datacenter electricity figure: 7,663 GWh in 2025, 23% of metered consumption (CSO). Nobody keeps a site register, so we built one from EPA industrial-emissions licences, EU ETS permits and planning appeals: 40 sites, 22 located, most of them from grid references inside the EPA documents themselves (per-stack coordinates in licence attachments, site-centre points on application forms, main-entrance references on older permits). Sixteen operating sites carry permitted standby generation: 3,830 MW thermal by licence cap, 4,279 by sum of units.

Generators are sized to carry a site's full design load, so permitted standby bounds load from above once the redundancy design is known. That design is the uncertain factor. Under N+1 the metered total bounds utilisation of permitted capacity at roughly 0.54–0.71 and plausibly puts it at 0.3–0.5; under 2N it imposes no bound. The one site with a published meter (Microsoft DUB14/15, 111.9 GWh in 2024, campus ramping) reads 0.12–0.23 depending on how its gas and diesel layers are counted.

What this gives a datacenter subsector: a located site list in a metered country, a permit-derived capacity ceiling per site, and a national total that any set of per-site estimates must sum to. What it does not give: a utilisation factor. The redundancy design has to be read from the licence operational reports, which is the next step.

Virginia: the permits, extracted

Virginia DEQ publishes a list of every issued datacenter air permit. On 21 October we opened all 183 permit PDFs and extracted the standby generator inventory from each, then read every permit a second time with an independent method and resolved the disagreements by hand: 182 sites, 13,973 permitted standby generator units, 33,693 MW, 177 sites at high confidence, median site 129 MW, largest 1,331 MW; 180 with a site address in the resolved file, 126 geocoded. Site-average unit size runs 0.4 to 3.7 MW, median 2.5, with most units at 2 to 3 MW. The first automated reading had given 19,186 MW; the second reading found it low in 98 of 107 disagreements, traced most of the gap to one parser bug (the first row after each table header dropped) and the rest to six smaller patterns, and resolved every case against the permit text. The comparison, the resolutions and the error patterns are in the package.

Dominion reported about 40 GW of datacenter capacity “in various stages of contracting” as of December 2024. Standby is sized to full design load with redundancy, so 33.7 GW permitted implies a design load of roughly 28–33 GW under N+1 and about half that under 2N; permitted is not built, and the 40 GW is one utility’s pipeline while the permits cover the state. The two figures are of the same order. That is the Ireland check in the one region where you would pilot: a public ceiling per site, from a document, that any estimate has to respect, and that was itself checked twice.

This is the field, call it α, that your facility Scope 2 approach would need for datacenters and that no satellite produces.

Method, in one paragraph

Site emissions = load × [α · EFdedicated + (1 − α) · EFgrid], reported as a range with a confidence rating. That is your 5.11.0 facility Scope 2 pattern with one added field. Load must come from a signal independent of the supply document (permit generator MW, interconnection MW, register IT load, footprint), never from the supply MW itself; dedicated supply counts only from the year it is physically in service; the result is Scope 2, non-additive to your power-sector rows. We drafted five worked rows and withdrew them after review because they broke the first of those rules. The method note and the review are both in the package.
