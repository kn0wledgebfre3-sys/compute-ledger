# Public-power datacenter loads: what is public, what is not, and what to request

Compiled 2026-10-23 from web search and page fetches only (no browser). Every figure in the CSV carries the URL and the line read. "Derived" figures (e.g. aMW x 8,760) are marked as ours and are not in any document.

Companion CSV: `public_power_transcribed.csv` in this folder.

## 1. Memphis Light, Gas & Water (MLGW) and TVA — xAI Colossus

**What exists publicly (record):**
- MLGW "xAI Update" of May 5, 2025 (https://www.mlgw.com/images/content/files/pdf/new/5-5-25%20xAI%20Update.pdf) and an undated "2025 xAI Update" (https://www.mlgw.com/images/content/files/pdf/new/xAI%202025%20Update.pdf): Paul R. Lowry Rd site energized at 150 MW (8 MW from pre-existing substation + 142 MW from new substation built at xAI expense); second 150 MW increment requested (300 MW total) with second substation under construction, "pending TVA approval"; Tulane Rd site request "260MW to 1.1GW", unstudied; signed TVA/xAI/MLGW curtailment agreement.
- MLGW "Utility Support to xAI" deck to City Council MLGW Committee, July 9, 2024 (https://memphistn.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/xAI-water-and-electric-7-9-24-final.pdf): 8 MW existing, 50 MW by 1 Aug 2024 at $760,000 MLGW cost, developer-paid 150 MW substation (~$24M), "This project will add approximately 150MW to those peaks", MLGW normal peaks ~2,000 MW winter / ~3,000 MW summer.
- MLGW "xAi Project Quick Facts" (2024, two versions): 50 MW interim, 150 MW total request, TVA Board approval needed for 150 MW.
- TVA Board resolution + memo, Nov 7, 2024, "Greater than 100 MW firm power arrangement with CTC Property LLC (xAI)" (URL in CSV): approves 150 MW firm power to MLGW's new customer; memo states "By August of this year, xAI was already operating at over 50 MW with a combination of limited MLGW power and xAI gas turbines." This is the single strongest record on xAI's load.
- TVA "Power Availability" resolutions Feb 11, 2026 and Aug 20, 2026: both anonymized ("a local power company (LPC) served customer", "greater than 100 MW"); the Feb 2026 one references a "Confidential Memorandum dated January 26, 2026". Press (WSMV Feb 12, 2026) attributes the Feb 2026 approval to xAI's additional 150 MW.

**What does not exist publicly:**
- No metered MWh or billing-demand figure for xAI anywhere. All figures are capacity made available or requested.
- MLGW Board of Commissioners minutes checked (May 7, 2025; Sept 17, 2025; June 4, 2025 preliminary agenda) contain no xAI resolution or MW figure — consistent with the substations being built at xAI expense, so there was no MLGW procurement resolution to vote on. Only a public comment "regarding xAI" (May 7, 2025).
- No TVA-posted document for the second 150 MW names xAI; the Jan 26, 2026 memo is withheld as confidential.

**Records request — MLGW:**
- Addressee: MLGW Public Records / Legal Department, Memphis Light, Gas and Water Division, 220 S. Main St, Memphis TN 38103 (check mlgw.com for the current custodian email).
- Statute: Tennessee Public Records Act, Tenn. Code Ann. § 10-7-503 et seq. (requester must be a Tennessee citizen under § 10-7-503(a)(2)(A) — use a Tennessee-resident requester). Expect MLGW to cite § 10-7-504(a)(20) (utility customer personally identifying information) and/or contract confidentiality; argue it applies to individuals, not a 150 MW industrial account.
- Ask by name for: (1) the executed Electric Service Agreement / Power Contract between MLGW and CTC Property LLC for 3231 Paul R. Lowry Rd, including contract demand (kW) and any curtailment/demand-response exhibit; (2) the MLGW and TVA "system impact studies" for the first and second 150 MW requests at Paul Lowry (named as such in the May 5, 2025 update); (3) monthly billing demand (kW) and energy (kWh) for the CTC Property LLC accounts from June 2024 to date; (4) the Tulane Rd "finalized request for grid power" and any system impact study; (5) the Electrolux-substation upgrade work order referenced as "$760,000" in the July 9, 2024 deck.

**Records request — TVA (FOIA):**
- Addressee: TVA FOIA Officer, 400 W. Summit Hill Dr., WT 7D, Knoxville TN 37902; foia@tva.gov; TVA FOIA regs 18 CFR Part 1301.
- Ask by name for: (1) the "Confidential Memorandum dated January 26, 2026" filed as Exhibit 02/11/26E to the Feb 11, 2026 Power Availability resolution; (2) the memorandum dated July 22, 2026 filed as Exhibit 08/20/2026P to the Aug 20, 2026 Power Availability resolution; (3) the TVA "term sheet" and demand-response agreement with CTC Property LLC/xAI referenced in the Oct 7, 2024 memo; (4) TVA's system impact study for the 150 MW and second 150 MW requests at the former Electrolux site. Expect Exemption 4 (confidential commercial) redactions; the Nov 2024 memo shows TVA has already released the 150 MW figure, which undercuts a blanket withholding.

## 2. CPS Energy (San Antonio)

**What exists publicly (record):** "Managing Large Load Growth Responsibly", Board informational update, March 4, 2026 (URL in CSV): queue table as of 01/12/2026 — 59 projects / 26,146 MW requested; 335 MW contracted; 1,411 MW in process; "Commercial = 56% of electric system sales with 9% from large load"; forecast "~630 MW" large-load growth in 5 years, "~1,500 MW in 10 years"; large load = 19% of total expected. Earlier: "Data Center & Large Load Presentation", May 30, 2024 ("approximately 1 GW currently in queue"). Large-load threshold is 40 MW (cpsenergy.com/largeload).

**What does not exist publicly:** No document names Microsoft (Texas Research Park / Westover Hills) or CyrusOne, and no MWh by data-center class. The "9% from large load" is a share of commercial-class sales (14,500,000 MWh) — derived ≈1,305 GWh, our arithmetic.

**Records request:**
- Addressee: CPS Energy Public Information / Open Records, 500 McCullough Ave, San Antonio TX 78215 (openrecords@cpsenergy.com or via the City of San Antonio PIA portal).
- Statute: Texas Public Information Act, Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552. CPS will invoke § 552.133 (public power utility competitive matters) and § 552.104/552.110; the Attorney General ruling process under § 552.301 follows.
- Ask by name for: (1) the list of "Contracted" large-load projects (335 MW as of 01/12/2026) with customer name, site address and contracted MW, as underlying the March 4, 2026 Board deck; (2) the Large Load Interconnection Study (LLIS) and End-User Interconnection Study reports for any Microsoft Corporation or CyrusOne site; (3) annual kWh and peak billing kW for 2023–2025 for accounts on the Large Power / Extra Large Power rate at Microsoft's Westover Hills and Texas Research Park addresses.

## 3. Silicon Valley Power (City of Santa Clara)

**What exists publicly (record):**
- "AI Data Center Power Supply and Impacts", N. Procos, NCPA Annual Conference, Sept 25, 2025: "55 Data Centers"; "60% / Power Use and Sales are from Data Centers"; "714 MW Peak Demand"; "Data Center usage increased 4% from FY2023"; "Most new data centers request 50–99 MW"; "request to add 500+ MW load (growing from 700 MW)".
- "SVP 101" deck (FY2024): sales by class — Industrial 4,181,000 MWh (91.6%), Total 4,560,000 MWh; peak 714 MW.
- APPA article (press) of Jan 2026 testimony: 55%, ~60 data centers, peak ~740 MW, projecting ~1,300 MW.
- CEC 2024 IEPR data-center forecast uses SVP's per-site history (5 MW year-1 assumption, peak ≈67% of installed capacity) but publishes no SVP-only table.

**What does not exist publicly:** No "load by customer class" table with a data-center row, no top-10 customers list, no per-customer MW. Derived: 60% × 4,560 GWh ≈ 2,736 GWh (ours).

**Records request:**
- Addressee: City Clerk, City of Santa Clara, 1500 Warburton Ave, Santa Clara CA 95050 (public records portal), copying SVP Electric Utility Director.
- Statute: California Public Records Act, Gov. Code § 7920.000 et seq. Expect § 7927.410 (utility customer usage data) to be raised; it permits disclosure where the public interest clearly outweighs, and SVP already publishes the 60% aggregate.
- Ask by name for: (1) the FY2024 and FY2025 "Summary of Sales" by rate schedule (kWh and peak kW) for the data-center rate schedules/large-industrial schedules, as used to produce the "60% of power use and sales" slide; (2) the "Actual Demand vs. Agreement Demand (in MW)" series shown in the Sept 25, 2025 NCPA deck, with underlying values by year; (3) the "Under Contract / In Negotiations / In Pipeline (MWh)" load-growth table from the same deck; (4) SVP's list of the ten largest customers by kWh with annual kWh (the standard "ten largest customers" table in the City's electric revenue bond official statements — check EMMA at emma.msrb.org for Santa Clara Electric Revenue Bonds, which usually prints this table).

**What exists publicly:** Only class aggregates. LES 2025 financial report: industrial 477,804 MWh, peak 769 MW, 423 MW wind "to support Lincoln's new data center" (supply, not load). NPA 2025 Load & Capability Report never says "data center". OPPD 2025 Annual Report/Operations Review: no data-center mention.

**What does not exist publicly:** Any MW/MWh for Meta Papillion, Google Papillion/Omaha, Microsoft, or Google Lincoln. Press: WOWT (Nov 6, 2024) "19 written requests from data centers" to OPPD; Flatwater Free Press (Mar 2026) Google proposal "1,000 to 3,000 megawatts" from leaked district documents.

**Records request:**
- OPPD: Corporate Secretary / Public Records, OPPD, 444 S. 16th St Mall, Omaha NE 68102. NPPD: Public Records, 1414 15th St, Columbus NE 68601. LES: Records, 1040 O St, Lincoln NE 68508.
- Statute: Nebraska Public Records Statutes, Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 84-712 to 84-712.09; expect § 84-712.05(3) (trade secrets/proprietary commercial information) to be cited; 4-business-day response under § 84-712(4).
- Ask by name for: (1) OPPD's annual "Large Customer" or "Top 10 customers" kWh report, and the list of ≥1 MW service requests and the "19 written requests from data centers" (2024) with requested MW; (2) the Board of Directors "All Committees" or "Energy Production & Delivery" presentations that gave the load-forecast slides attributing growth to data centers, with MW; (3) the electric service agreements with Meta Platforms (Papillion), Google LLC (Papillion/Omaha) stating contract demand; (4) LES: the electric service agreement and contract demand for Google LLC's Lincoln site, and the "proposal documents" for the 1,000–3,000 MW site referenced by Flatwater Free Press.

## 6. Grant County PUD (Washington)

**What exists publicly (record):** "Grant PUD & Data Centers: FAQs" (Aug 28, 2026): 8 data-center operators, ~280 aMW in 2025 of ~757 aMW system (37%); ~800 MW in queue. Commission recap Aug 27, 2024: 2,897 MW requested by 75 applicants, data centers 1,568 MW, Quincy 1,578 MW. Commission recap Mar 25, 2025: growth caps per data-center customer. 2022 Annual Report has no data-center line. Derived: 280 aMW ≈ 2,453 GWh (ours).

**What does not exist publicly:** Per-customer MW (Microsoft Quincy etc.), rate-schedule-level kWh.

**Records request:**
- Addressee: Public Records Officer, Public Utility District No. 2 of Grant County, P.O. Box 878, Ephrata WA 98823 (publicrecords@grantpud.org).
- Statute: Washington Public Records Act, RCW 42.56. RCW 42.56.330(2) protects residential customer data only; RCW 42.56.270 may be cited for financial/commercial information supplied in connection with contracts — contest it.
- Ask by name for: (1) 2024 and 2025 retail load by rate schedule (aMW, MWh and peak kW) including Rate Schedule 7 (Large Industrial), 14 and 17; (2) the monthly load (kWh and peak kW) for each of the "8 data center operators" named in the Aug 28, 2026 FAQ, by customer; (3) the Large Load queue list (applicant, location, requested MW) as of the latest date; (4) Power Sales Agreements / Electric Service Agreements with Microsoft Corporation (Quincy) stating contract demand and the "megawatts they have left to grow" allocations from the Mar 25, 2025 commission action.

Bonus — Chelan County PUD (same statute): June 2, 2025 and June 16, 2025 commission decks on the Microsoft Malaga contract state "Chelan PUD surplus power (up to 18 MW)" but no total contract demand; request the executed Microsoft power sales agreement and Exhibit on contract demand (minutes of June 4, 2025 at https://www.chelanpud.org/docs/default-source/commission/minutes-06-04-25.pdf).

## 10. Germany — EnEfG data-centre register

**Legal basis (note numbering):** EnEfG § 13 (Informationspflichten; Abs. 1 obliges operators to publish Anlage 3 indicators per data centre), § 14 (Energieeffizienzregister für Rechenzentren, run by the federal government), Anlage 3 (data points). The task's "§12/§13" corresponds to §§ 13/14 in the enacted text (AlgorithmWatch, 17.04.2026, cites "§13 Absatz 1" and "Energieeffizienzregister für Rechenzentren gemäß §14 EnEfG").

**Portals:**
- Public register: https://rechenzentrumsregister.bund.de/de/ ; annual reports list at https://rechenzentrumsregister.bund.de/de/annual-reports/ — both returned robots_disallowed to the fetcher, so no site rows could be read in this session. Must be opened in a browser.
- Reporting portal (operators): https://rzreg.bmwk.de ; info page: https://www.bundeswirtschaftsministerium.de/RZReg/rechenzentrums-register.html (describes three public areas "Wissen", "Statistik" (interactive Germany map of aggregated data) and "Jahresmeldungen" (individual reports "sofern deren Betreiber eine Veröffentlichung über das Öffentliches RZReg gewählt haben")). Contact rzreg@bafa.bund.de.

**What is public per site (from UBA, 21.09.2026, and netzpolitik.org, 22.08.2026):**
- Register published by BMWE on 14 Aug 2026; 319 data centres have published data for the current reporting year ("Für das aktuelle Berichtsjahr sind Daten von nur 319 Rechenzentren veröffentlicht"); netzpolitik: 482 reported 2025 consumption data as of early July; ~2,000 DCs in Germany, ~1,000 likely reportable; large international operators largely missing.
- Per-site PUE is readable (UBA computed distributions: ~70% ≤1.5, ~40% ≤1.3, ~7% ≥2.0). UBA also computed renewable coverage (~80% claim 100% balance-sheet renewables) and IT vs total connected load ratio (12% of entries have IT load ≥ total load), implying Anschlussleistung (IT and total) and renewable share are visible per site. Reporting duty (≥300 kW) covers consumption, connected load, efficiency, renewable supply and water use, but publication via the register is optional ("keine Pflicht, dies über das Rechenzentrumsregister vorzunehmen") and some reported data "unterliegen aber keiner Veröffentlichungspflicht, und sind somit nicht zugänglich".
- Whether absolute annual electricity consumption (kWh) is shown per site could not be verified here; AlgorithmWatch recommends operators "absolute Zahlen wie den Gesamtstromverbrauch individuell zu veröffentlichen", which suggests it is not consistently published. ERF: not mentioned by UBA.
- Pending amendment (BMWE draft 9.4.2026): adds a trade-secret exemption from publication (§ 13 Abs. 1 Satz 2 EnEfG-E).

**Records request — Germany:** Antrag nach § 1 Informationsfreiheitsgesetz (IFG) and, in the alternative, § 3 Umweltinformationsgesetz (UIG) (energy consumption as environmental information) to Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Energie (BMWE), Referat RZReg, and to BAFA (register operator, rzreg@bafa.bund.de), asking for: the complete Anlage 3 Jahresmeldungen for Berichtsjahr 2024 and 2025 per Rechenzentrum (Betreiber, Standort, Gesamtstromverbrauch kWh, IT-Stromverbrauch, nicht redundante Nennanschlussleistung, PUE, ERF, Anteil erneuerbarer Energien, Wasserverbrauch) including those whose operators did not opt for publication; UIG § 9 balancing applies to any trade-secret claim.
