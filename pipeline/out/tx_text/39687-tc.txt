Process Description
Lone Star Power Station is a natural gas-fired simple cycle power generation unit located in San
Jacinto County. The primary source of criteria pollutants is one natural gas-fired combustion
turbine (CTG). Lone Star Power Station’s net electrical generation is a nominal 450 megawatts
(MW) of electricity.
The CTG is equipped with dry low nitrogen oxide (NO ) combustion systems to control NO
x x
emissions and is fired exclusively with pipeline-quality natural gas which minimizes emissions of
volatile organic compounds (VOCs), particulate matter, and sulfur dioxide. The turbine is also
equipped with selective catalytic reduction (SCR) for control of NO , and an oxidation catalyst to
X
minimize carbon monoxide (CO) and VOC emissions.
Other emission sources include lube oil vents, a dewpoint heater, an emergency diesel
generator, an emergency diesel fire water pump, diesel tanks, natural gas condensate tanks,
piping fugitives, and maintenance, startup, and shutdown (MSS) activities.
