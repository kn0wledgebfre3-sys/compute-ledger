    Totals that can be added

    Adding figures from different documents can mislead: a national figure counted again with its own sites, two years mixed, a claim added to a record, the same site counted twice. The ledger adds figures only where it can say exactly what the sum is. Under each total it lists the figures added and every figure of the same kind, unit and period it left out (about a site in the country, the country, or a company registered there, records and claims alike), with the reason, and counts the figures with another definition or for another period. Each total is the sum of what the records the ledger holds give for those sites. It is not a total for the country, and the row says how many of the country's sites in the ledger it covers.

    The rules

    A figure is added to a total only when all six hold.

    1. Sites only. A figure for a country, a company or an entry may already include the sites, so it is never added to them.
    2. Records only. A claim is not added, nor a figure the ledger computed, nor one known only through a press report.
    3. Exact values in one unit. An approximate value, a lower or upper bound, a range, or a value its document says is partly or wholly estimated is not added; values are added in the ledger's standard unit, and each figure as printed is in the list.
    4. The same span of time. Every figure covers the same stated period, and none is flagged by its document as covering less of it. A count or limit as of a date is not added.
    5. The same definition. The same kind of figure, described in the same words, in the same country. Figures described differently are kept apart, even where the difference may be only wording.
    6. One figure per site. Where a site has two such figures, neither is added. A total needs at least 2 sites.
