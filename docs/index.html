What is not published

Building scrapers for these sources showed where the public record stops:

  • Texas's grid operator publishes no file of its large-load queue. The totals appear only as numbers printed in monthly reports and board slides, with no breakdown by county or project.
  • Great Britain's system operator stated in a freedom-of-information response that it does not keep a register of demand connections. The nearest public list covers only applicants who agreed to be listed.
  • The EU's datacenter reporting database is behind an operator login. Only an aggregate report is public; there is no per-site data.
  • Texas's environmental regulator lists datacenter air-permit applications only while they are pending, and the state's datacenter register (the Comptroller's tax-exemption list) has no addresses. Once a permit is issued, the generator inventory sits in a records system that needs a browser. The ledger rebuilt Texas from EPA's facility registry and TCEQ permit notices instead: 129 facilities, generator inventories for two.

Scripts that pull the queues of PJM, MISO, SPP and NYISO, US federal plant and fuel data, and the British figures exist and pass offline tests; their live numbers will appear here once run. The Virginia DEQ permit extraction (all permits on the statewide list, read twice) was completed on 21 October 2026 and the Texas rebuild on 22 October; the Virginia figures are in the Virginia generator entry, and both are in the Climate TRACE handover package in the repository.

Method

What counts as a record

A document produced by a regulator, court, grid operator, statistical office, auditor, UN body, customs authority, or a legally bound corporate filing. A company's sustainability report is a claim, not a record, unless a figure is third-party assured and the assurance statement is public.

What a gap means

A one-line statement of where the record and the claim differ, written without adjectives. "None documented" means the record and the claim agree or no claim was found. "Positive example" means the record shows a practice worth copying.

Confidence and verification

High means the figures were read in the primary document or a mirror of it. Medium means at least one key figure comes from a press report of the document. Low means the figure is second-hand, from a company statement, or older. A second pass went back to the original document for every medium and low entry and for some high ones; an entry with no verification tag was not re-checked in that pass. 'Verified against primary' means the original confirmed the figures; 'partly verified' means it confirmed some but not a key figure; 'primary not found' means the note says what was searched and what was missing. Mirrors of the actual document (a law firm's copy of a regulator decision, a library copy of a UN report) count as primary; company press releases and law-firm summaries do not. Figures the ledger produced itself from public documents (the Virginia permit extraction) link to the extraction files, which link to each document.

What is excluded

Estimates the ledger would have to make itself, modelled footprints, advocacy press releases cited on their own, and anything without a URL to the underlying document. Estimates published by a public body or the cited author (a market monitor's counterfactual, an auditor's projection, a grid operator's forecast, a tracking study's extrapolation) are included and labelled as estimates.

Corrections

Anyone with contributor access can submit a correction on an entry with a source. The correct response to an entry is a newer document, not a rebuttal. Entries change when the record changes, and every entry carries a last-checked date.

Scope

Datacenters, the grids and water systems they sit on, the public money and land they use, and both ends of the hardware chain: the mines and fabs upstream, the recyclers and dumps downstream. Labor appears where a court or ministry has recorded it.
