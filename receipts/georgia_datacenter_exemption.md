# Receipt: Georgia data center sales and use tax exemption, and the January 2026 DOAA job revision

Jurisdiction: United States, Georgia. Last checked: 2026-10-20.
Ledger entries: `us-ga-doaa-exemption-correction` (people_land_money.json), `us-ga-georgia-power-large-load-certification` (north_america.json).

Marking used below: **[press]** means the figure or date was confirmed only through a news report and not in a primary document. **[arithmetic]** means the ledger computed a ratio from two published figures; no estimate is involved.

---

## 1. Summary

Georgia exempts purchases of high-technology data center equipment from state and local sales and use tax under O.C.G.A. § 48-8-3(68.1). The exemption was enacted in 2018 (HB 696). In 2022 HB 1291 moved the end of the exemption period to 31 December 2031. An evaluation prepared for the Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts (DOAA) by the University of Georgia's Carl Vinson Institute of Government was released on 24 December 2025 [press]. It estimates FY2025 forgone state revenue at $474,182,904 and attributes 30% of data center activity to the exemption. DOAA's summary of the evaluation reported 28,350 construction jobs and 5,471 operations jobs [press]. On 14 January 2026 [press], DOAA reissued the summary marked "Revised January 2026" with 8,505 and 1,641 jobs. Both revised figures are 30% of the originals [arithmetic]. Forgone revenue was unchanged, and the summary gave no reason for the change. In March 2024 a fiscal note signed by the same State Auditor had put FY2025 forgone state revenue at $50.2 million. A 2024 bill to pause the exemption was vetoed. A 2026 repeal bill passed the Senate but did not pass the House [press]. Over the same period the Public Service Commission (PSC) certified 9,885 MW of new generation, most of it for large new customers. By March 2026 Georgia Power reported 12,400 MW of committed large-load customers.

## 4. The gap

1. The DOAA summary of 24 Dec 2025 reported 28,350 construction jobs and 5,471 operations jobs for 2025 [press]. The revised summary of 14 Jan 2026 reports 8,505 and 1,641, which are 30.0% of the earlier figures [arithmetic], while forgone FY2025 revenue stays at $474.2 million. (T14, T15)
2. The original job and value-added figures match the totals of Tables 3 and 4 of the evaluation report, which give statewide 2025 data center construction and operations impacts. The report's own 30% "but for" table (Table 5) carries value added only, and its 2025 operations figure of $247,004,097 matches the revised summary's $247.0 million. (T12, T14, T15)
3. The revised summary does not say what was changed or why, and no DOAA document stating a reason was found. As checked on 20 Oct 2026, the copy of report 33298 on Open Georgia is not marked revised and still shows 28,350 and 5,471 in Tables 3–4. (T12, T15, T16)
4. The fiscal note signed by the State Auditor on 14 Mar 2024 put FY2025 forgone state revenue from the existing exemption at $50.2 million. The evaluation DOAA released on 24 Dec 2025 puts FY2025 forgone state revenue at $474,182,904. (T5, T11)
5. The 2022 evaluation attributed 90% of Georgia data center activity to the exemption, and the 2025 evaluation attributes 30%. (T4, T11)

## 5. What the record does not show

- **The original summary text.** The 24 Dec 2025 summary was not retrieved. Its figures are confirmed only through press: [Capitol Beat, 14 Jan 2026](https://capitol-beat.org/2026/01/error-in-state-auditors-data-center-review-inflated-job-production-and-economic-value/), [The Center Square via The Augusta Press, 27 Dec 2025](https://theaugustapress.com/report-30-of-georgias-data-centers-traced-to-2018-tax-break/) and [WABE/Capitol Beat, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.wabe.org/state-report-says-data-centers-a-boon-to-economy-despite-tax-giveaway/). No archived copy could be reached.
- **A reason for the revision.** No statement from DOAA or the Carl Vinson Institute was found. Press reports state that none was given.
- **Measured cost.** The $474,182,904 FY2025 figure is a model estimate. The report says 2018–2024 values draw on Department of Revenue information and 2025–2030 values are estimates ([report 33298](https://open.ga.gov/openga/report/downloadFile?rid=33298), pp. 4–5).
- **A common scope for the two cost figures.** The [2024 fiscal note](https://opb.georgia.gov/document/fiscal-notes-2024/lc-50-0792s/download) covers equipment purchases and does not mention construction materials. The [2025 report](https://open.ga.gov/openga/report/downloadFile?rid=33298) describes computer equipment and software (p. 4) and refers to building materials (p. 13). No document reconciles $50.2 million with $474.2 million.
- **Jobs actually created.** No public count of jobs created at certified data centers was found. The report's employment figures are model outputs. The only enforcement it describes is a performance bond tied to the investment minimum, not to jobs ([report 33298](https://open.ga.gov/openga/report/downloadFile?rid=33298), p. 13).
- **The number of certified projects.** The record does not state it. The report says the Department of Revenue supplied data on 34 data centers that were using or had applied for the exemption ([report 33298](https://open.ga.gov/openga/report/downloadFile?rid=33298), p. 3/4).
- **One sunset date.** [HB 1291](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2022-signed-legislation/hb-1291/download) ends the exemption period on 31 Dec 2031 and repeals it on 1 Jan 2032, and the [2024 veto message](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2024-vetoed-legislation/2024-veto-messages/download) says the extension runs through 2031. The [2025 report](https://open.ga.gov/openga/report/downloadFile?rid=33298) states 2033 (p. 12), and no document reconciles these.
- **How many committed customers are data centers.** The [Q1 2026 Large Load report](https://services.psc.ga.gov/api/v1/External/Public/Get/Document/DownloadFile/226607/107887) does not say how many of its 31 committed customers are data centers. The [stipulation](https://services.psc.ga.gov/api/v1/External/Public/Get/Document/DownloadFile/223391/104616) states no data center MW figure.
- **Jobs delivered by announced projects.** The [Facebook (2018)](https://nathandeal.georgia.gov/press-releases/2018-03-07/deal-facebook-invest-750-million-stanton-springs-data-center) and [AWS (2025)](https://aboutamazon.com/news/aws/aws-investment-georgia-ai-cloud-infrastructure) announcements do not mention the exemption. No record of jobs delivered at those sites was located.
- **Activity attributable to the exemption, in industry figures.** Industry-commissioned totals measure all data center activity. The PwC study for the Data Center Coalition reports 30,070 direct and 176,790 total Georgia jobs for 2023 [press: [Capitol Beat, 3 Jul 2025](https://capitol-beat.org/2025/07/impact-of-data-centers-in-georgia-a-mixed-bag/)]. The PwC report itself was not retrieved.
- **Final legislative status from primary sources.** The House outcome for SB 410 is confirmed only through press. Bill status pages and bill texts on legis.ga.gov could not be read.

## 6. Method

- **Starting points.** The Georgia sections of `people_land_money.md` and `north_america.md`, and registry entries `us-ga-doaa-exemption-correction` and `us-ga-georgia-power-large-load-certification`.
- **Retrieval.** URLs were found by web search, and each document was read through a page fetch. The fetcher returns extracted text, not page images. Direct downloads were blocked, so no PDF was downloaded or checked page by page.
- **Cross-checks.** Each key figure in DOAA report 33298 was requested two or three times with differently worded questions, and the figures matched every time. Printed page numbers drifted by one between fetches (for example, Table 3 came back as p. 29 once and p. 30 once), so the timeline gives table numbers as the primary locator and both page readings where they differed.
- **Ledger arithmetic.** Ratios between published figures (8,505 / 28,350; 1,641 / 5,471; $1,254.4M × 4%) are marked [arithmetic]. No figure was estimated.
- **Quotes.** One short phrase at most per source. Everything else is paraphrase.
- **Not fetchable** (permission timeout or unreadable):
  - DOAA summary landing page `audits2.ga.gov/reports/summaries/georgia-data-center-sales-use-tax-exemption/`
  - `audits.ga.gov/ReportSearch/download/33298` (the copy of report 33298 on DOAA's own domain; the Open Georgia copy was used)
  - Wayback Machine capture of the original December summary
  - DOAA summary page for the 2022 evaluation
  - LegiScan HB 1192 (2023–24)
  - Capitol Beat's December 2025 story (read through WABE's republication)
  - PwC/DCC study landing page (centerofyourdigitalworld.org)
  - PSC staff testimony (FACTS documentId 224483)
  - legis.ga.gov bill-text API documents (returned binary)
  - PSC file 226607/107927 (returned binary)
- **Corrections to the ledger registry found while building this receipt:**
  - The north_america registry entry quotes the stipulation as saying the forecast "depends largely on one customer class with limited historical data". The fetched text says the forecast is largely driven by anticipated growth from one customer class that lacks historic information (Supply Side ¶2, p. 1), so the registry wording is a paraphrase, not a quotation.
  - The people_land_money entry records `claim: none found`. This receipt supplies the claim.
