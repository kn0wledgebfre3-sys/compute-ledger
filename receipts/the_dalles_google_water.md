Marking used below: **[press]** means the figure or date was confirmed only through a news report and not in a primary document retrieved for this receipt. Where the press report attributes the figure to City of The Dalles records, the row says so. **[arithmetic]** means the ledger computed a ratio from two published figures; no estimate is involved.

## 1. Summary

Google has operated data centers in The Dalles since 2006 on and near the site of a former aluminum smelter. On 24 September 2021 a reporter for The Oregonian requested Google's water-use records from the city. The city denied the request as a trade secret, the Wasco County District Attorney ordered disclosure on 15 October 2021, and on 29 October 2021 the city sued the reporter and the newspaper in Wasco County Circuit Court (Case 21CV42306) to block release, with Google responsible for the city's costs under its agreements. On 8 November 2021 the city council approved, 5–0, a $28.5 million water-infrastructure agreement with Google's subsidiary Design LLC for two new data centers, under which Google transfers groundwater rights tied to the smelter site to the city and the city supplies an undisclosed volume of water. On 14 December 2022 the city settled: it released ten years of Google's annual use (2012–2021), agreed to future disclosures, and paid $53,000 toward the newspaper's legal costs, which Google reimbursed along with the city's own $106,000 [press]. The released records show Google's use rising from 104.3 million gallons in 2012 to 355.1 million gallons in 2021, 29% of the city's consumption [press: City records as reported]. City records reported by OPB show 383.9 million gallons in 2023 and 434.4 million gallons in 2024, about one third of the city's water [press]. In November 2022 Google began publishing site-level water figures and compared its global use to 29 golf courses. In October 2025 Google transferred a completed aquifer storage and recovery system and the smelter groundwater rights to the city and said this gives the community 100 million more gallons a year. In January 2026 Google said it had offset its local use through watershed investments and was not behind a federal bill to transfer 150 acres of national forest to the city for a reservoir expansion from 900 to about 3,000 acre-feet. City officials dispute OPB's framing and say the expansion is not for Google. No document in the record reconciles Google's replenishment claims against the city-metered volumes.

## 4. The gap

1. The volumes became public only through a settlement filed 14 December 2022, thirteen months after the city sued the reporter, with Google reimbursing the city's $106,000 and the $53,000 paid to the newspaper's counsel [press]; the council had approved the $28.5 million agreement on 8 November 2021 with Google's projected use confidential. (T5, T7, T9, T11, T15)
2. City records as reported put Google at 355.1 million gallons and 29% of city consumption in 2021 and 434.4 million gallons and about one third in 2024, a rise of 22% in volume [arithmetic]; Google's statements in October 2025 and January 2026 describe replenishment and offsets without a Dalles-specific volume, and no document in the record sets the replenished volume beside the metered use. (T16, T22, T24)
3. For 2021 Google's site-level disclosure is 274.5 million gallons while the city's record is 355.1 million gallons; for 2024 Google's reported withdrawal is 461.1 million gallons while the city's record is 434.4 million gallons [press]; the documents use different metrics (consumption, withdrawal, city-metered delivery) and none reconciles them. (T14, T16, T21, T24)
4. Google says the aquifer storage and recovery system and the rights transfer give the community 100 million more gallons a year; the watermaster's March 2025 briefing described ASR testing as ongoing with limited volume, and the record contains no measured recovered volume. (T19, T22)
5. The 2024 master plan includes an unnamed industrial user of about 1 MGD; OPB reports Google at about 1.19 MGD in 2024; the city says Google is not that user; no document identifies the user. (T18, T24, T25)
