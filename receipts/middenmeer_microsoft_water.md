Marking used below: **[press]** means the figure or date was confirmed only through a news report and not in a primary document. **[arithmetic]** means the ledger computed a ratio or difference from two published figures; no estimate is involved. Volumes are given as published; 1,000 m³ = 1 million litres.

## 1. Summary

Microsoft operates hyperscale data centers on the Agriport A7 business park at Middenmeer, in the municipality of Hollands Kroon, and holds a permit for two further buildings (AMS13 and AMS14) at Cultuurweg 11. In June 2021 Microsoft gave a drinking-water figure of 12 to 20 million litres a year, which the municipal executive repeated to residents and councillors [press]. In April 2022 Microsoft reported to the municipality that the site had used 84 million litres of drinking water in 2021, split as 75 million for operations and 9 million for facilities, and the municipality changed the figures on its website [press]. The Noordhollands Dagblad published the 84 million figure on 11 August 2022, eight days after the Minister of Infrastructure and Water Management had reported a national "feitelijk watertekort" (actual water shortage) to parliament. Microsoft's responses, all reported through press, were that the earlier figure described use at the time, that the 2021 volume included construction water, that a mature site is on track for about 10 million litres a year, and that 36 million litres were discharged back, leaving net consumption near 48 million litres. On 4 October 2022 the Tweede Kamer adopted a motion citing the 84 million and 12–20 million figures and asking the government to examine a ban or cap on drinking-water use in hyperscale permits. The Omgevingsdienst Noordzeekanaalgebied granted the AMS13/14 permit on 19 April 2023; press reports of the permit give an evaporative air-cooling system and 40 × 3.3 MW plus 2 × 1 MW backup generators but no water volume. From 1 January 2024 a national instruction rule confines new hyperscale data centers to parts of Hollands Kroon and Het Hogeland. No metered drinking-water figure for Middenmeer for any year after 2021 was found in a public document.

## 4. The gap

1. The figure given in June 2021 (12,000–20,000 m³ a year) and the figure Microsoft reported to the municipality in April 2022 for calendar 2021 (84,000 m³) describe the same site; 84,000 / 20,000 = 4.2 and 84,000 / 12,000 = 7.0 [arithmetic]. Both figures are confirmed only through press. (T9, T16, T21)
2. Microsoft's net figure of 48 million litres (84 million withdrawn less 36 million discharged) is 2.4 to 4.0 times the June 2021 range [arithmetic], and the 10 million litre figure for a mature site has no published metered year against which to check it. (T24, T30)
3. The 75,000 m³ component was described as operations (April 2022 email), as cooling (to the newspaper, August 2022, and per Computable, October 2022), and as operation and construction (to Computer Weekly, November 2022); no single Microsoft document reconciles these descriptions. (T16, T21, T29, T30)
4. The Tweede Kamer motion adopted on 4 October 2022 states that the 12–20 million litre estimate was in Microsoft's permit application; Microsoft said it was not, Grinwis accepted that point, and the municipal permit documents retrieved for this receipt (the 2020 Nota van zienswijzen and the January 2021 notice) contain no drinking-water volume. (T3, T4, T28, T29)
5. Documents released under the Wob request contained an engineering-firm estimate above 100,000 m³ a year made for Microsoft before the 12–20 million figure was circulated; the municipality had denied that estimate, and the Wob documents were not located. (T21)

## 6. Method

- **Starting points.** Registry entry `nl-middenmeer-microsoft-water` (europe.json) and its verification note, and the Netherlands section of `research_notes/Global AI compute ledger/europe.md`.
- **Retrieval.** Searches were run in Dutch and English with WebSearch; each document was read with WebFetch, which returns extracted text, not page images. Shell network access was blocked, so no PDF was downloaded or checked page by page. Primary documents reached: Tweede Kamer papers on officielebekendmakingen.nl and app.1848.nl (AH 2300; Kamerstukken 26 643 nrs. 819, 900, 911, 919, 939; 27 625 nr. 586), the 4 Oct 2022 voting record (via eerstekamer.nl), Staatsblad 2023, 492, Provinciaal Blad 2024, 531, Gemeenteblad 2021, 7972, the Hollands Kroon Nota van zienswijzen, three Noord-Holland GS documents (Statenvragen nr. 22 of 2024, the 2021 datacenterstrategie memo, the October 2022 withdrawal of the reactive directive), the Datacenterstrategie 2025–2027, the Raad van State ruling of 19 June 2024, the BZK beslisnota of 26 May 2023, the KNMI summer 2022 overview, and Microsoft's own meeting report, 2023 fact sheet and 2025 localized document.
- **The municipal document itself could not be fetched.** hollandskroon.nl and a Wayback Machine capture of it timed out awaiting permission, and no raadsinformatie letter with the 2021 volumes surfaced in search. The 84 million, 75 million, 9 million, 12–20 million, 100,000 m³, 10 million, 36 million and 48 million litre figures are therefore marked [press].
- **Cross-checks.** The 84 million litre figure appears consistently across six press reports and the adopted motion; the split 75/9 appears in DCD, Computable and Computer Weekly. The permit chronology (5 Jan 2021, 2 Mar 2021, 3 Aug 2021, 15 Sep 2021, 17 Feb 2022, 11 Apr 2022, 8 Jul 2022, 19 Apr 2023) is from the GS answers of 2 April 2024 and matches the BZK beslisnota and press.
- **Ledger arithmetic.** Ratios 84,000 / 20,000, 84,000 / 12,000, 48 / 20 and 48 / 12 are marked [arithmetic]. No figure was estimated.
- **Quotes.** One short phrase at most per source. Everything else is paraphrase.
- **Not fetchable** (permission timeout or not located):
  - `hollandskroon.nl/datacenters` and its Wayback Machine capture
  - `loket.odnzkg.nl/actuele-bekendmakingen/` (OD NZKG permit portal; the 19 Apr 2023 decision for Cultuurweg 11)
